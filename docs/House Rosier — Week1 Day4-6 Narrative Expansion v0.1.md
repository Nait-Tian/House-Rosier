# House Rosier — Week1 Day4–6 Narrative Expansion v0.1

## Goal

Day4–6 now form the bridge between recovery and the Day7 Rosecourt Spring Garden Party.

- Day4: the player learns how dangerous the rumors around the Rosier succession have become.
- Day5: Lucien and Elise help the player prepare a public self rather than imitate the old Nait; Damien introduces the political guest list.
- Day6: Damien, Alaric, and Roderic each receive a separate pre-party interaction that can seed attraction without route locking.

## Day4 — The Rumor Becomes Real

The player sees letters from allies, neutrals, and houses testing the succession situation. Lucien confirms that the Spring Garden Party will proceed under its traditional name, while everyone understands its real function: proving that House Rosier still has a functioning heir.

Choices track whether the player prefers direct political information, trusts family filtering, or focuses on practical action. A second conversation asks whether the player first notices personal loyalty, political opportunity, or undecided neutral houses.

Early Week1 choices can already echo here through Elise's dialogue.

## Day5 — Preparing the Public Self

Elise and Lucien prepare the player's clothing and presentation. The player can wear an old heir pin, a newly chosen white-rose clasp, or the formal Rosier crest. These are presentation choices, not identity locks.

Lucien establishes a core principle: the player does not need to lie on behalf of the old Nait. Memory and judgment are not the same thing.

Damien visits with an annotated guest list. The player can ask for strategy, ask where Damien places himself politically, or tease him about finding an excuse to visit. This creates political-trust and personal-trust seeds separately.

Day1–3 exploration choices can change preparatory narration.

## Day6 — Three Private Impressions

### Damien

A short rehearsal tests how the player would respond to genuine allies asking about health. Damien explicitly promises not to answer for the player during the party unless asked.

### Alaric

Alaric explains that rumors are shifting from physical illness toward magical or mental discontinuity. He asks how the player would respond if challenged to prove they are "the same Nait." The answer concerns method, not identity stance.

Prior Alaric choices can affect how he frames the question: he remembers honesty, evidence-first reasoning, and asserted boundaries.

### Roderic

A familiar horse reacts to Nait, producing an unresolved body-memory clue. Roderic refuses to treat this as proof of identity. The player can restart from basics, trust the body once, respect current physical limits, or joke about the horse having better memory.

Earlier recovery and body-memory choices are directly called back.

### Elise Night Scene

Elise reminds the player that tomorrow's guests have not lived through the previous six days. The player may express readiness, nervousness, or ask her to stay close.

## Callback Principle

Day4–6 should not only create new flags. They should interpret Day1–3 choices as continuity, growth, or contradiction.

The player remains free to change. Earlier decisions affect how characters understand current behavior rather than restricting current choices.
